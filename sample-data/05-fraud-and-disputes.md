# Fraud, Unauthorised Transactions and Disputes

This procedure covers electronic transactions the customer did not authorise, and
disputes about transactions the customer did authorise.

## Reporting channels

Unauthorised transactions must be reportable around the clock. The bank provides
a toll free number, an in-application report option, and a dedicated email
address. A reference number and the time of reporting are issued immediately and
are the basis for calculating liability.

On report, the card or the channel involved is blocked at once, before any
investigation begins.

## Customer liability for unauthorised electronic transactions

Liability depends on the cause and on how quickly the customer reported it.

**Bank fault.** Where the loss arises from negligence, a system deficiency or a
breach within the bank, the customer bears nothing, regardless of whether the
customer reported it.

**Third party breach, neither bank nor customer at fault.** Liability depends on
the delay between receiving the bank's transaction alert and reporting.

| Delay in reporting | Customer liability |
| --- | --- |
| Within 3 working days | Nil |
| 4 to 7 working days | Lower of transaction value or 25,000 |
| Beyond 7 working days | Per board approved policy, up to full value |

**Customer negligence.** Where the customer has shared credentials, a one time
password or a card personal identification number, the customer bears the entire
loss until the moment of reporting. Losses after the report are borne by the bank
regardless of the cause.

## Shadow credit and resolution timelines

A provisional credit, called a shadow credit, is applied to the account within
ten working days of the report. It is applied whether or not the investigation
has concluded and is not contingent on establishing the cause.

Investigations are closed within ninety days of the report. Where the
investigation finds the customer bears no liability the shadow credit is made
permanent. Where liability rests with the customer the credit is reversed with at
least seven days notice in writing.

If the bank fails to conclude within ninety days, the shadow credit is made
permanent irrespective of the finding.

## Card disputes and chargebacks

A disputed card transaction where the customer accepts they made the purchase is
handled as a chargeback through the card network, not under the unauthorised
transaction rules.

Common grounds and their evidence requirements:

**Goods or services not received.** Customer must confirm the expected delivery
date has passed and that the merchant was contacted. Raise after fifteen days
from the expected delivery date, within one hundred and twenty days of the
transaction.

**Goods not as described or defective.** Requires a description of the
discrepancy and evidence that return was attempted.

**Duplicate processing.** Two identical amounts to the same merchant on the same
day. Resolved quickly since the network data is conclusive.

**Cancelled recurring payment.** Requires the date of cancellation and how it was
communicated. Frequently fails where the merchant's terms required notice the
customer did not give.

The merchant has thirty days to respond. If the merchant provides compelling
evidence, the case proceeds to pre-arbitration and then arbitration, where the
network decides. Arbitration carries a fee that is borne by the losing party, so
low value cases are usually written off rather than escalated.

## Fraud patterns seen most often

**Remote access fraud.** The customer is persuaded to install a screen sharing
application by someone claiming to be from technical support or from the bank.
The bank never asks a customer to install such software.

**Fraudulent reward or refund messages.** A message claims the customer's reward
points are expiring or a refund is pending, linking to a page that captures
credentials. The bank never sends links requesting card details.

**Collect request abuse.** The victim is asked to approve a payment request while
being told it will credit money to them. Approving a collect request always debits
the approver.

**Customer support number substitution.** Fraudsters place false contact numbers
online. Only numbers printed on the card and on the official website are genuine.

## What the bank never does

The bank never asks for a personal identification number, a password, a one time
password, a card verification value, or the full card number. No employee, at any
level, has a legitimate reason to request these. Any such request is fraudulent
regardless of how convincing the caller appears.
