# Know Your Customer and Account Onboarding

Every account relationship requires identification and verification before the
first credit is permitted. This procedure applies to branch, assisted and fully
digital onboarding.

## Officially valid documents

Any one of the following establishes both identity and address: passport,
driving licence, voter identity card, job card issued by NREGA signed by a state
officer, or the letter issued by the National Population Register containing
name and address.

The permanent account number is collected separately for tax reporting. Where a
customer does not hold one, Form 60 is obtained instead.

Where the document presented does not carry the current address, a separate
proof of current address is obtained. Utility bills not more than two months old,
municipal tax receipts, and registered lease agreements are accepted.

## Verification methods

**In person at a branch.** The original document is seen, a copy is taken, and
the copy is stamped as verified against the original by the attending officer,
who records their employee code.

**Video based identification.** A trained officer conducts a live video call
from bank premises. The session must capture the customer's face and the original
document simultaneously, confirm liveness through an unscripted action, and
capture the geolocation to confirm the customer is within India. The recording is
retained. Sessions are subject to concurrent audit.

**Digital verification through a central identity service.** Used where the
customer consents and provides the relevant identifier. Only the last four digits
are retained in the core banking record; the full identifier is never stored in
any bank system.

## Risk categorisation

Every customer is assigned a risk category at onboarding and reviewed
thereafter.

Low risk covers salaried individuals with verified employment and stable
transaction patterns. Review every ten years.

Medium risk covers self-employed individuals, professionals and small business
proprietors. Review every eight years.

High risk covers politically exposed persons and their close relatives,
non-resident customers from jurisdictions flagged by the financial action task
force, customers dealing in cash-intensive trades such as bullion or scrap, and
customers whose beneficial ownership could not be established with confidence.
Review every two years.

## Periodic re-verification

Re-verification is due on the schedule above. Where there is no change in
information, a self-declaration through internet banking, mobile banking, email
from the registered address, or a letter is sufficient. A branch visit is not
required and no charge may be levied.

Where the address has changed, the new address is captured with a declaration and
verified within two months. Failure to complete re-verification results in the
account being restricted to credits and withdrawals only, with no fresh
facilities, after two written notices and a thirty day grace period.

## Accounts that cannot be opened

An account is refused where the customer declines to provide required
documentation, where identity cannot be verified, where the name matches a
sanctions list and the match is confirmed on review, or where a decisive
beneficial owner cannot be identified for a non-individual applicant.

Anonymous or fictitious name accounts are prohibited without exception.

## Nomination

A nominee is offered at onboarding and the customer's choice, including a refusal,
is recorded. Nomination can be added, changed or cancelled at any time at no
charge. Only one nominee is permitted per deposit account.

## Minors

An account for a minor below ten years is operated by the guardian. From ten
years the minor may operate a savings account independently, subject to a
withdrawal limit of 25,000 per day and no overdraft or cheque book facility. On
attaining eighteen years, fresh specimen signatures and identification are
obtained and the operating mandate is updated.
