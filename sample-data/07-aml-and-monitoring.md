# Anti-Money Laundering and Transaction Monitoring

Obligations arise under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act and the rules made
under it. This note describes what is monitored, what is reported, and the
timelines.

## Reports filed with the Financial Intelligence Unit

**Cash Transaction Report.** All cash transactions above ten lakh, and series of
connected cash transactions below that threshold which together exceed ten lakh
within a calendar month. Filed by the fifteenth day of the following month.

**Suspicious Transaction Report.** Any transaction which gives rise to a
reasonable ground of suspicion, whether or not it involves cash and whatever the
amount. There is no monetary threshold. Filed within seven working days of the
suspicion being established by the designated director.

**Counterfeit Currency Report.** Any instance of forged notes encountered. Filed
by the fifteenth day of the following month.

**Cross Border Wire Transfer Report.** All inbound and outbound wire transfers of
five lakh or equivalent and above.

## Tipping off is prohibited

Neither the filing of a report nor its contents may be disclosed to the customer
or to any third party. This applies to all staff without exception. A customer
asking whether a report has been filed is told only that the bank complies with
its statutory obligations.

Because of this, an account may be restricted without the customer being told the
underlying reason.

## Indicators that trigger review

An alert does not imply wrongdoing. It requires a human to look.

**Structuring.** Multiple deposits just below a reporting threshold, particularly
across different branches or on consecutive days.

**Pass-through activity.** Credits followed promptly by transfers out leaving a
negligible balance, with no economic rationale visible in the customer's profile.

**Activity inconsistent with the declared profile.** A salaried customer with
declared income of eight lakh a year receiving crores in credits.

**Sudden dormancy reversal.** An account inactive for over a year suddenly
receiving high value credits.

**Round tripping between related parties.** Funds circulating among accounts with
common signatories, addresses or telephone numbers.

**High risk geography.** Counterparties in jurisdictions identified as having
strategic deficiencies by the financial action task force.

**Cash intensity inconsistent with trade.** A business whose sector is normally
non-cash depositing predominantly in cash.

## Enhanced due diligence

Applied to high risk customers. The source of funds and, where relevant, the
source of wealth are documented rather than merely asserted. Beneficial ownership
is traced to natural persons holding more than 10 percent for companies and 15
percent for partnerships and unincorporated bodies.

Politically exposed persons require approval from senior management before the
relationship is established, and annual review thereafter regardless of the
general review cycle.

## Sanctions screening

Customers are screened at onboarding and on every update, and the full customer
base is rescreened whenever a designated list changes. Payments are screened in
real time before release.

A positive match stops the payment and is escalated the same day. Where a match is
confirmed, funds are frozen and the matter reported; funds are not returned to the
remitter, since returning them would itself breach the prohibition.

Name matching produces false positives at a high rate because transliterated
names collide. Every match is reviewed by a human, and an automated release is
never permitted.

## Record retention

Transaction records are retained for ten years from the date of the transaction.
Customer identification records are retained for ten years from the end of the
relationship. Records relating to a filed report, or to any ongoing investigation
or proceeding, are retained until the matter concludes even if the ten years has
elapsed.

## Governance

A designated director at board level and a principal officer are appointed and
their names filed with the regulator and the financial intelligence unit. The
principal officer is responsible for deciding whether a suspicion is established
and for filing.

An independent audit tests the monitoring rules annually, including whether alerts
are being closed with adequate reasoning. Alert closure without a recorded
rationale is treated as an audit failure.
